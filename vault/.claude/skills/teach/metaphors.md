# One-line metaphors (shared by /teach, /homework, /guide, /exam-prep)

By default metaphors are **general and everyday** (setup can switch this to comparisons from {{NAME}}'s own life in `About Me.md`, or turn them off).

## Rules
1. **One line, inside the lesson, never instead of it.** The lesson keeps everything it had: the textbook content, steps, visuals, checks. The metaphor is one extra sentence that makes the idea stick.
2. **One per new idea.** Each new concept or refresher gets one; follow-ups on the same idea don't need another. Leave it out if nothing simple truly fits, since a forced metaphor is worse than none.
3. **Everyday and instantly clear.** Use things anyone knows: money, shopping, cooking, sports, driving and speedometers, maps, phones, weather, queues, mail, school life. It should make sense in about 3 seconds with no extra explanation. No jargon inside the metaphor.
4. **Accurate.** The structure has to really match (rate ↔ speedometer, bias ↔ a scale that reads heavy). If it breaks somewhere important, say where in a few words ("…except a derivative can be negative").
5. **Rotate** images across lessons so they don't blur together.
6. **Track what lands:** if a metaphor makes something click, note it in the Learner Model's "clicked via" (e.g. `metaphor: speedometer`) and in `Learning Profile.md` §3.

## The quality bar (examples)
- **Calc, derivative:** "f′ is f's speedometer: not where you are, but how fast you're changing right now."
- **Calc, +C:** "Knowing your speed for the whole trip tells you how far you went, not where you started. The +C is the starting point."
- **Calc, chain rule:** "Gears: if A spins 3× as fast as B and B spins 2× as fast as C, A spins 6× as fast as C. Rates multiply."
- **Calc, (ln u)′ = u′/u:** "u′/u is a percent change: a $1 raise is huge on a $10 allowance and tiny on a $1,000 paycheck."
- **Stats, bias vs sample size:** "A bathroom scale that reads 5 lb heavy: weighing yourself 100 more times just gives the wrong number more confidently."
- **Stats, confounding:** "Ice cream sales and sunburns rise together, but summer is causing both."
- **Stats, random assignment:** "Shuffling before you deal, so nobody can stack the teams."
- **Physics, work / dot product:** "Pushing a stroller by its slanted handle: only the forward part of your push moves it forward."
- **Physics, work–energy:** "Net work is deposits and withdrawals on a kinetic-energy bank account: your push deposits, friction withdraws."
- **Haskell, pattern matching:** "Sorting mail by the shape of the envelope: the shape decides which slot it goes in."
- **Haskell, recursion:** "Nesting dolls: open one, handle it, repeat on the smaller one until you hit the solid doll in the middle (the base case)."
- **French, subjunctive:** "*Il faut que* flips a switch: once it's on, the verb after it changes mode."
- **Piano, practice:** "Practice the hard bar alone, like shooting free throws instead of playing a whole game to fix your shot."
