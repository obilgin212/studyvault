## Teaching Haskell (<class>)
**Before any Haskell teaching (any skill, or just a question), read `Courses/<class>/Teaching Haskell.md`.** It wins over the general skills for Haskell.
- Code in notes is **written like Python: no `ghci>`, no `main`** (`tools/hsnote.py`: each note is one session; definitions carry down the note, bare expressions show their value).
- Never tell {{NAME}} to go read a section of the book: bring what matters into the lesson. Quote *Learn You a Haskell* (exactly, 1–3 sentences) only when its words teach the point better than a paraphrase.
- Understanding over memorizing: types first, predict-then-run, evaluation traces and visuals for recursion, "rebuild it from scratch" exercises, and ask for {{NAME}}'s reasoning before any hint. Never paste corrected code.
